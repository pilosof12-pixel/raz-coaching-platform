# Cluster N — Advanced Gymnastics, Calisthenics & Weighted Street Lifting

## Purpose
This cluster is an INTERNAL coaching decision layer for advanced bodyweight skills and weighted street lifting. It complements the exercise library and existing programming-method articles. It does not permit invented exercises. Every printed exercise must still be a canonical library entry or an explicitly verified route in the skill graph.

The engine must distinguish three different problems before programming:

1. Skill acquisition: leverage, balance, straight-arm or transition skill is the limiter.
2. Maximal strength: the target is a very high 1RM or low-rep weighted expression.
3. Loaded endurance: the target is many clean reps with a fixed external load.

Do not use the same progression model for all three.

---

# Article N1 — Universal Advanced Skill Progression Rules

## Core model
Advanced bodyweight skills are not a single linear ladder. Build them through parallel routes:

- specific holds or full-pattern exposures
- leverage regressions and progressions
- eccentric exposures
- verified assistance methods
- dynamic strength through the same family when relevant
- prerequisite strength and position control

The specific skill remains the goal. Assistance work supports it but does not replace direct exposure once the athlete is capable of a measurable, tolerable version.

## Readiness thresholds
Readiness numbers are coaching heuristics, not physiological laws. If the athlete already demonstrates a cleaner harder progression than the nominal threshold suggests, use observed ability over an arbitrary gate.

Quality beats duration. A longer hold with bent elbows, lost scapular position, large pelvic break, or uncontrolled leverage does not qualify as progression evidence.

## Anti-hallucination rule
Never create a new variation by adding words such as wall-supported, banded, assisted, eccentric, partial, deficit, one-leg, tuck, straddle, or negative to an existing skill unless the complete variation is explicitly verified in the exercise library or skill graph.

If no verified regression exists, select a verified supporting exercise rather than inventing one.

## Programming order
When skill quality is a primary goal, perform the highest-skill exposure after warm-up and any genuinely low-fatigue neural primer, before fatigue-producing strength or hypertrophy work.

Do not use a primer that fatigues the same limiting tissues enough to lower skill quality.

## Fatigue rule
Stop or regress skill work when position quality deteriorates meaningfully. For advanced static or high-skill work, accumulating technically poor attempts is not productive volume.

# Article N2 — Planche Progression and Programming

## Technical standard
A planche exposure requires locked elbows, strong scapular protraction, controlled depression appropriate to the athlete's structure, posterior pelvic tilt, active glutes and a rigid body line. Do not count a hold that is maintained mainly by elbow bend or large lumbar extension.

## Base route
1. Planche Lean
2. Tuck Planche
3. Advanced Tuck Planche
4. Straddle Planche, optionally using a VERIFIED light-band straddle route to learn the line
5. Full Planche, with verified assisted or eccentric work when appropriate

## Practical readiness anchors
- Planche lean: roughly 20 seconds of high-quality position is a useful readiness marker for tuck work.
- Tuck planche: roughly 10 to 15 seconds of clean control is a useful marker before meaningful advanced-tuck exposure.
- Advanced tuck: roughly 10 to 15 seconds of clean control supports meaningful straddle work.
- Straddle: build repeatable quality exposures; clusters such as 5 x about 5 seconds can be useful. A strong roughly 15 to 20 second straddle indicates substantial readiness for full-planche specific work.

These are heuristics. Do not block a harder progression that the athlete can already perform cleanly.

## Support routes
Use verified pseudo-planche pressing, planche push-up variants, eccentrics, and band assistance only when they preserve the intended body line and are real named movements in the library/graph.

# Article N3 — Front Lever and Dragon Flag Progression

## Front lever route
1. Tuck Front Lever
2. Advanced Tuck Front Lever
3. One-Leg Front Lever
4. Straddle Front Lever
5. Full Front Lever

Support the static route with verified front-lever raises and eccentrics. Raises can progress through tuck, advanced tuck, straddle and full forms only when those variants are explicitly represented in the library/graph.

Do not treat a raise as a substitute for all static exposure when the athlete's goal is a static lever.

## Dragon flag route
Use the same principles of leverage and eccentric control:

1. bent-knee or shortened-lever control when needed
2. straddle eccentric
3. controlled straddle concentric/eccentric reps
4. long-lever or legs-together eccentric
5. full strict concentric/eccentric dragon flag

Do not confuse spinal position failure with productive overload. Stop a rep when the athlete can no longer preserve the intended trunk position.

# Article N4 — Human Flag Progression

## Readiness base
Useful practical indicators include a strong vertical pulling and pressing base plus lateral trunk control. Approximately 15 strict pull-ups, about 20 controlled dips, and a clean straight-arm side-plank hold around 30 seconds can support readiness, but they are not absolute gates.

## Skill route
1. verified tuck eccentric exposure
2. Tuck Human Flag hold
3. verified one-leg eccentric exposure
4. One-Leg Human Flag hold
5. verified straddle eccentric exposure
6. Straddle Human Flag hold
7. verified full eccentric exposure
8. Full Human Flag hold

Do not create wall-supported flag variations unless a specific variation has been verified and entered in the database.

Treat top-arm pulling, bottom-arm pushing and lateral trunk stiffness as separate potential limiters when selecting support work.

# Article N5 — Handstand, HSPU and 90-Degree HSPU

## Three-route model
The engine must separate balance skill, vertical pressing strength and 90-degree transition strength.

### Handstand control route
Build a stable freestanding handstand independently. Freestanding HSPU work should not be prescribed as if pressing strength alone solves balance.

### Pressing strength route
A practical progression is:

1. pike / elevated pike pressing as needed
2. back-to-wall HSPU base
3. belly-to-wall HSPU and deeper-ROM/deficit belly-to-wall strength
4. freestanding HSPU eccentric
5. freestanding HSPU

A strong back-to-wall set, roughly 8 to 10 quality reps, can justify greater belly-to-wall emphasis. Around 7 to 8 strong belly-to-wall reps plus stable freestanding handstand control can justify meaningful freestanding eccentric work. These are heuristics, not absolute gates.

### 90-degree route
The 90-degree HSPU requires both a strong deep vertical press and control through the bent-arm transition. Build:

- deep/deficit HSPU strength
- freestanding balance and HSPU control
- verified 90-degree eccentric transition work
- verified partial transition work where the partial is deliberately defined and library-approved
- full 90-Degree Handstand Push-Up

Never prescribe a '90-Degree Wall HSPU' as a regression. That is not an approved route.

# Article N6 — One-Arm Pull-Up and Unilateral Pulling Skill

## Distinguish strength base from specific skill
Weighted pull-up strength can create useful surplus, but it does not by itself teach one-arm coordination.

Use verified routes only, selected from:

- heavy weighted pull-up / chin-up strength base
- assisted one-arm pull-up where the exact assistance method is verified
- unilateral pulldown or other support work as capacity work, not as a direct OAP substitute
- eccentric OAP
- isometric OAP at defined joint angles
- partial OAP when the partial range is deliberate and standardized
- full One-Arm Pull-Up

## Side-specific pain routing
A painful side does not automatically cancel the healthy side. Preserve low-volume specific exposure on a healthy side when appropriate. On the symptomatic side, choose the highest useful, verified exposure that is tolerated, such as greater assistance or a lower-load unilateral pulling variant. Do not map a pain score directly to a numerical 'joint load score'. Use symptom character, severity, movement tolerance, next-day response and performance quality.

# Article N7 — Ring Straight-Arm Strength: Iron Cross and Related Skills

## Governance
Extreme ring strength has high connective-tissue and technical demands. Use a prerequisite and graded-exposure model rather than chasing the final position early.

For Iron Cross and later Maltese/Victorian development:

- require stable ring support and straight-arm control
- establish adequate pulling and pressing strength surplus
- use only verified assisted cross positions and eccentrics
- progress assistance gradually while preserving elbow lock and shoulder control
- avoid adding multiple new high-tendon-load straight-arm elements in the same block

Maltese, Victorian and Manna remain conservative until their family-specific graphs have been fully verified.

# Article N8 — Weighted Street Lifting: Maximal Strength vs Loaded Endurance

## First classify the target
A weighted pull-up or dip goal must be classified by its expression:

### Maximal-strength expression
Examples: highest possible 1RM, heavy single, double or triple.

Programming priorities:

- the competition or goal movement is the anchor lift
- heavy work uses long enough rest to preserve rep quality
- keep most heavy work short of grinding failure; use true maximal attempts selectively
- use low-rep back-off volume to maintain enough practice and tissue exposure
- reduce secondary pulling/pressing volume before sacrificing anchor-lift quality
- use small load jumps as the athlete becomes advanced

Max-effort methods can be useful when the target is maximal strength, but the engine must not force a Westside template. Select the method based on athlete history, goal specificity and recovery.

### Loaded strength-endurance expression
Example: 20 clean pull-ups with +30 kg.

This is NOT a 1RM problem. The engine must preserve enough maximal strength that +30 kg represents a manageable fraction of the athlete's capacity, while separately building high-quality volume at and around the goal load.

Use a two-track model:

1. Strength reserve track: low-rep heavier weighted pull-ups/dips.
2. Specific endurance track: accumulated clean reps at the goal load or a nearby load using straight sets, ladders, clusters or density blocks.

Dose endurance from CURRENT capacity at the relevant load whenever that benchmark is available. Do not anchor sessions to the future target number.

Clusters are appropriate when the athlete needs high-quality accumulated reps while avoiding the technique breakdown of one long set. Straight sets and ladders are appropriate when repeated-set endurance itself is the limiter.

## Dynamic effort
Dynamic-effort work is a tool for rate of force development and explosive intent, not a mandatory ingredient for every weighted pull-up or dip program. Use it when acceleration/explosive pulling is a relevant limiter, especially for high pulls or muscle-up transfer. Do not substitute speed work for the heavy specificity required by a maximal-strength goal.

## Recovery and freshness
Do not encode 'CNS freshness' as a mystical score. Protect high-quality heavy sessions using observable proxies:

- unexpected loss of rep speed or explosiveness
- repeated performance drop on the anchor lift
- deterioration of technical quality
- unusually high perceived effort at familiar loads
- sport load and local tissue soreness
- insufficient recovery since the previous high-stress pulling or pressing exposure

Longer spacing between high-stress exposures can be appropriate for advanced athletes, but there is no universal fixed number of days. The athlete's response governs.

# Article N9 — Exercise Ordering, Priming and Method Selection

## Ordering hierarchy
When multiple qualities share a session, default to:

1. technical skill requiring freshness
2. low-fatigue neural/ballistic primer when it demonstrably supports the priority task
3. primary maximal-strength or power anchor
4. secondary strength work
5. hypertrophy/accessory work
6. conditioning or density work when compatible with the day

This hierarchy is conditional. If a primer reduces performance on the priority movement, remove it.

## Method routing
Choose the method because it solves the athlete's limiter:

- Max effort: near-maximal strength expression and heavy coordination
- Dynamic effort: explosive intent / rate of force development
- Repetition work: hypertrophy, local capacity and weak-point volume
- Cluster/rest redistribution: preserve quality across higher total work
- Eccentric: specific strength exposure when full concentric expression is not yet available or when eccentric capacity is the intended target
- Isometric: position-specific strength and static-skill exposure
- Density/ladders: rep endurance and work-capacity expression

Never prescribe a method merely because it exists in the knowledge base.

# Article N10 — QA Rules for Elite Bodyweight and Street-Lifting Programs

Before shipping a program with an advanced bodyweight or weighted-street-lifting goal, verify:

1. The exact goal expression was classified correctly: skill, maximal strength, loaded endurance, or mixed.
2. Every named skill variation exists in the library or verified graph.
3. No modifier-generated exercise was invented.
4. The athlete receives direct exposure to the named goal when currently capable and tolerable.
5. Support exercises address a real limiter rather than replacing specificity.
6. Static-skill quality criteria are explicit.
7. Eccentric, assisted and partial routes are verified, not improvised.
8. High-tendon-load straight-arm work is not stacked recklessly.
9. Max-strength and loaded-endurance pull-up goals use different programming logic.
10. Fatigue management uses observable performance and recovery signals rather than an unsupported fixed 'CNS' number.
11. Sport sessions are counted when they share grip, elbow-flexor, shoulder, trunk or systemic recovery channels.
12. Multiple compatible primary goals may progress together when recovery and interference permit; do not arbitrarily force only one progression quality.
