Here is your four-week program designed to improve your 3 km run, 10 km ruck, and strict pull-ups, while maintaining your strength benchmarks. This program integrates your current running and rucking schedule with three gym sessions per week, carefully managing impact and fatigue across five training days to prevent shin splint irritation.

We'll use your current performance markers as anchors, progressing one main variable at a time for your running and rucking to ensure sustainable improvement. Your gym sessions will prioritize your pull-up goals and maintain your squat and deadlift strength, with lighter, recovery-focused work on Fridays.

**Progression Notes for Weeks 2-4:**
* **Run Intervals:** We will gradually increase either the total work volume or the pace of your intervals, but not both simultaneously, to allow for adaptation and minimize shin impact. Week 4 will feature a slightly faster pace with reduced volume for a controlled speed expression.
* **Backpack Carry:** We'll maintain the 20 kg load and progressively increase either the distance or the pace of your ruck, focusing on improving your 10 km time.
* **Strict Pull-ups:** You'll progress by increasing the reps or sets for your strict pull-ups, and by increasing the load or reps for your weighted pull-ups.
* **Strength Lifts (Squat, Deadlift, OHP):** These will be maintained at a challenging but submaximal RPE (7-8) to preserve strength without excessive fatigue, allowing recovery for your running and rucking.
* **Accessories:** Reps or load will gradually increase on movements like Bulgarian Split Squats to support your lower body strength and resilience.

**Pain and Substitution Guidance:**
* **Shin Splint Irritation:** If you experience any shin pain, immediately reduce the intensity or volume of your running and rucking. Focus on easy, low-impact aerobic work (e.g., cycling if available, or very easy walking) and ensure your strength sessions, especially lower body, are pain-free. Do not push through sharp or increasing pain.
* **General Pain:** For any other pain, first try reducing the load or reps, or adjusting the range of motion. If pain persists, substitute the exercise with a pain-free alternative that targets a similar muscle group or movement pattern. For example, if Back Squats are painful, try Leg Press or Goblet Squats at a comfortable range.
* **Recovery:** Listen to your body. If you feel excessively fatigued, especially before a hard running or rucking session, prioritize rest or reduce the intensity/volume of the planned workout. The Friday gym session is designed to be low-cost and recovery-contingent, so feel free to shorten it or remove an accessory if needed.
