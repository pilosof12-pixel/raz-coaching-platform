# Tactical Selection Prep — 3K Hybrid stress avatar

Canonical short name: **Tactical 3K**

Purpose: adversarial coaching/product QA for a tactical-hybrid client. This is not an official Navy SEAL preparation standard. The avatar intentionally combines a primary short-distance running goal with loaded movement, calisthenics, strength retention, finite session time and a mechanical-load history so the engine must prioritize rather than simply add more hard work.

## Intake

- Age: 27
- Sex: male
- Body mass: 82 kg
- Experience: intermediate/advanced general training
- Programmed training days: 4 per week
- Session ceiling: 60–75 minutes
- Training location/equipment: full gym, track/road access, hills, pull-up bar, 20 kg ruck/backpack, 30 kg sandbag, sled
- Primary goal: improve 3 km from 13:30 to sub-12:00 over the longer training horizon. The current 4-week block should move toward that target without pretending 4:00/km is already continuously sustainable.
- Secondary goals: improve 10 km ruck with 20 kg from 95 min toward 82 min; improve strict pull-ups from 14 toward 18–20; maintain useful squat/deadlift strength; remain athletic and relatively weight-stable rather than bodybuilding-oriented.
- Current running: 3 sessions/week, about 18–20 km/week. Typical week: one interval session, one easy run, one longer aerobic run. Recent 400 m repetitions are around 1:42–1:45 with full enough recovery for repeatability.
- Current ruck: 1 session/week, usually 8–10 km with 20 kg. Current 10 km benchmark about 95 min.
- Current strength: Back Squat 140 kg x 5; Deadlift 180 kg x 3; Overhead Press 65 kg x 5; Weighted Pull-up +30 kg x 5; 14 strict bodyweight Pull-ups.
- Current push endurance: about 55 clean Push-ups in 2 minutes.
- Injury/load history: previous shin-splint irritation when running volume was increased abruptly. Currently asymptomatic at the present running and ruck volume.
- Recovery: typically 7–8 h sleep, generally good recovery.
- Preference: wants “combat-ready / special-operations-style” fitness, but does not want random punishment circuits, unnecessary mass gain, or conditioning that makes the 3 km worse.

## What a strong result must do

1. **3 km remains the primary performance target.** Preserve three direct running exposures unless the program explicitly explains a justified temporary trade-off.
2. **Use the current 13:30 3 km as the performance anchor.** Current race pace is 4:30/km. Do not prescribe a continuous non-test run faster than that pace for current-race duration or longer. Sub-12 goal pace is a future target, not a Week-1 continuous prescription.
3. **Not every run is hard.** At least one exposure should be genuinely low intensity. Faster work can use intervals/repetitions when source-appropriate and calibrated from current performance.
4. **Rucking is a named target modality, not generic GPP.** Preserve at least one direct ruck/loaded-march exposure per week. Farmer carries, sled work or sandbag carries may support it but must not silently replace it.
5. **Ruck progression is mechanically sane.** Do not simultaneously make the pack heavier, the distance longer and the pace faster. Do not default to heavy loaded running. Progress the smallest useful variable and respect foot/ankle/shin tolerance.
6. **Strength is retained efficiently.** Use enough squat/hinge/pull/press work to preserve useful force production without turning the block into a bodybuilding program or exhausting the legs before key running work.
7. **Pull-up goal keeps direct specificity.** Pull-ups/weighted pull-ups should remain direct work; machines may support but cannot replace the named calisthenics target.
8. **The shin history changes load management, not the entire identity of the program.** Avoid abrupt simultaneous increases in running impact and ruck stress while current tolerated exposure is maintained.
9. **No military-themed fatigue theatre.** Burpee punishment circuits, daily maximal tests and arbitrary “operator” finishers do not count as tactical specificity.
10. **The four-day / 75-minute ceiling is real.** The program may intelligently combine compatible work or consolidate stress, but should not hide a seven-day training plan inside four nominal days.
11. **Four-week progression is believable.** Progression should come from current capacity and the curated endurance/strength rules, not from writing the end goal into Week 4.
12. **Client workbook uses the same premium output standard as Youth gymnastics:** Overview, Week 1–4, Warm-Up, direct exercise/demo links, clear progression logic and usable notes.

## Deliberate failure traps

- Treating “combat ready” as permission to add more HIIT on top of a 3 km plan.
- Replacing a direct ruck with Farmer Carry/Sled Push because those look “tactical”.
- Prescribing 4:00/km as an easy/tempo continuous pace because it is the future 3 km goal pace.
- Cutting running below the athlete’s existing three weekly exposures without explanation.
- Increasing running volume and ruck load together despite the shin history.
- Four nominal sessions that each contain a full run, full strength workout and finisher, making the time ceiling fictional.
- Losing pull-up specificity in favor of generic rows/lat pulldowns.
- Turning strength maintenance into high-volume hypertrophy.

This avatar should be used after Youth gymnastics as a second permanent adversarial regression family because it stresses a different part of the system: endurance specificity, tactical-language interpretation, loaded locomotion, concurrent load management, and prioritization under a time ceiling.
