Here is your four-week program designed to balance your primary strength and calisthenics goals with your MMA training and marathon side quest. We'll prioritize your 220kg Back Squat and 4 One-Arm Pull-ups, while building your 100kg Overhead Press and maintaining your running base for the marathon.

The program is structured to manage fatigue from your 5 MMA sessions per week. We'll use a compact approach, focusing on quality over excessive volume, especially on days with sport. Progression will be steady, not aggressive, to ensure recovery.

**Progression Notes for Weeks 2-4:**
* **Back Squat:** We'll aim to increase load slightly or add reps within the prescribed RPE range, focusing on quality and recovery.
* **One-Arm Pull-up:** Progression will focus on increasing the quality or number of strict singles, and reducing assistance or increasing reps for Assisted One-Arm Pull-ups.
* **Overhead Press:** We'll work to increase load or reps for your direct strict Overhead Press, while the secondary press may vary to manage fatigue or provide a different stimulus.
* **Marathon Running:** Your long run will gradually increase in duration or distance, respecting your current weekly volume and recovery.
* **Accessories:** These are low-cost and will be adjusted or removed first if recovery or primary goal performance is compromised.

**Pain and Substitution Guidance:**
* If you experience any joint pain (especially elbows or shoulders with pulling/pressing, or knees/hips with squatting/running), reduce the load, reps, or range of motion immediately. Prioritize pain-free movement.
* For any exercise causing sharp or persistent pain, substitute with a less provocative variation or omit it and focus on recovery. For example, if Back Squats cause knee pain, try a Goblet Squat or Leg Press with a pain-free range. If One-Arm Pull-ups irritate your elbow, reduce assistance or switch to a very light Weighted Chin-up for a session.
* Listen to your body, especially with your high training load. It's always better to do less and recover than to push through pain and risk injury or overtraining.
