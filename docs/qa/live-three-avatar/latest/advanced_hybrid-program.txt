Built to keep the squat and One-Arm Pull-up as the highest-quality work while fitting around 5 MMA sessions. Monday is the main neural slot, Tuesday keeps the second One-Arm Pull-up exposure clean and lower-fatigue, Friday is deliberately low-cost around hard sport, and Sunday pushes strict press without blunting Monday.

Weeks 2-3 progress one lever at a time: Week 2 nudges squat load, assisted-One-Arm Pull-up assistance, and the long run distance; Week 3 keeps squat steady on purpose while the strict One-Arm Pull-up ladder lengthens, Sunday press gets heavier, and the long run extends once more. Week 4 trims sets by roughly a third while keeping moderate intensity so you finish fresher, not detrained.

If elbows or forearms flare on unilateral pulling, turn the strict One-Arm Pull-up day into all clean singles per arm and add 2.5-5 kg assistance on Assisted One-Arm Pull-up that week. If squat depth, bracing, or hip/back comfort degrades, repeat the last successful squat load and cut the final set. If Friday MMA fatigue is high, remove the final accessory first, then cut one set from Dumbbell Bench Press before touching Back Squat.

On Marathon: this block holds it at a maintenance dose rather than developing it, on purpose. 220kg back squat and 100kg overhead press are what this block develops, and 5 sport sessions a week already own most of the recovery budget. Keeping the exposure protects what you already have and keeps it ready to progress in the next block; it is not an attempt to improve it in these four weeks.
