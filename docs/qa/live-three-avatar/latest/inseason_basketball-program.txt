Match day is Wednesday and Saturday, and every session in this block is placed by its distance from match day. Two short gym sessions, both kept inside the match week instead of competing with it. Monday carries the only heavier lower-body strength exposure; Thursday is deliberately cheaper after the midweek game. No extra conditioning is added here because practice and matches already cover the hard repeat-effort load.

Weeks 2-3 only nudge one main lever at a time: a small trap bar load rise if the prior week stayed clean, and a slightly higher box only if takeoff and landing quality stay sharp. Week 4 is a consolidation week: less non-essential volume, moderate loads kept in, and the goal is to feel fresher while holding the same useful qualities. In season, holding strength and jump quality on this schedule is a win.

Patellar tendon rule: keep lower-body pain at or below 3/10 during the session and no worse than your normal next-morning baseline. If the split-squat row irritates it, shorten range and load first; if it still bites, skip that row for the day and keep the jumps, upper-body work and trunk. Heavy slow Back Squat stays out this block because that is the known flare trigger; the split-squat pattern covers the squat demand more safely this time of year.
