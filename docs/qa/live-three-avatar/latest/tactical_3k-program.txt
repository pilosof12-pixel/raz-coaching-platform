This block keeps the 3K first: 3 direct runs each week, 1 direct 20 kg ruck, and 3 genuine full-body strength days. Squat and deadlift stay on a low-fatigue maintenance dose, pull-ups get one weighted and one main bodyweight exposure, and the ruck is capped at 60 minutes here so it progresses by pace first rather than trying to force a full 10 km inside the time cap.

Weeks 2-3 lengthen the 3K work from 400s to 600s to 800s at the same current-calibrated rhythm while the easy running stays steady. Week 4 trims total run volume and uses faster 400s as a controlled speed expression; Wednesday pull-up volume builds through Week 3 then consolidates, and the ruck only tightens pace while 20 kg and 60 minutes stay fixed.

If shin soreness shows up during or within 24 hours of Tuesday intervals, first repeat the last clean interval week or drop one rep before touching the easy runs. If it shows up after Thursday ruck, hold or slow the ruck pace first while keeping 20 kg and no loaded running; if fatigue is high, cut Push-up or Calf Raise before cutting the key 3K session.

Weekly running volume anchor: your established, tolerated baseline is about 18-20 km per week across 3 running exposures, and this block keeps 3 running exposures while quality becomes more 3K-specific. Counting easy runs, warm-up, repetitions and cooldown, Week 1 projects roughly 14.2 km, which is deliberately below that baseline while the new interval specificity is the only impact variable stepping up. While shins and next-day soreness stay normal, rebuild toward the top of your 18-20 km range by adding a few minutes to the easy runs first, never by adding a fourth running day or by making the easy runs faster. If shin symptoms return, reduce the stressor that provoked them or the one most recently increased, the interval session if symptoms follow that session, the ruck if they follow loaded carrying, and easy-run volume only for diffuse accumulated soreness, then repeat the last symptom-free week before progressing again.
